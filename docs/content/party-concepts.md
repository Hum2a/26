The three dials start visually at 50 as a neutral control position, never as an asserted answer. Their endpoints are supplied comic opinions, not factual claims. Guess the Tab stores prices in pence but has no fabricated bounds, price or target. Its scoring needs explicit tie and all-guesses-over treatment; the ordinary slider's exact-value grading is not equivalent.

The existing ten `who-said-it-*` entries already cover concept 2. They were not duplicated or promoted. Their exact-source gaps remain documented in [the quotation audit](who-said-it-sources.md). The introductory “True equality…” line remains an example rather than an eleventh question.

## Reference checks

- Cheese rolling: the [event's media page](https://www.cheese-rolling.co.uk/media_press.html) describes racing after the cheese on Cooper's Hill. The question expressly asks whether the activity is real; “The Rollers” is the user's shorthand.
- Gurning: [Egremont Crab Fair's organiser history](https://egremontcrabfair.com/history.html) describes the face-pulling contest and the horse-collar frame. This supports the Cumbria activity.
- Muggletonians: the [British Library's manuscript catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/?q=036-001998704&sort=relevance) records Muggletonian treatises dating from the seventeenth century onward. Historian Ariel Hessayon's [research on the Ranters](https://arielhessayon.substack.com/p/the-ranters-part-two) discusses the historical name and the difficulty of treating its subjects as a single sect. The question asks about names used for movements rather than declaring an objective degree of heresy.
- Puddle-Stompers: the user explicitly supplied this as made up. No claim is made that every similarly named activity anywhere has been disproved.

These checks do not establish any of the four Guess the Tab prices. Those references need an agreed date and comparable basis before authoring a scored answer.
